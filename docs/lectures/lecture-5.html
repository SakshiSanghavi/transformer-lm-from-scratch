01 · Getting started with an NLP project

Dataset, evaluation, model: choose all three carefully

Dataset

Use an existing dataset or carefully curated data. Inspect licensing and source coverage. NLP training/evaluation data overrepresents English and some domains; dialects, code-switching, and many languages may have less support.

Evaluation

Start from prior work and established metrics. Use proper train/dev/test splits; use existing fixed splits when comparing with published work.

Model

Build a simple baseline first (for example majority class), then a stronger baseline. Tune both baseline and proposed model fairly.

Break down results

Performance may differ for frequent vs. rare entities, short vs. long inputs, language varieties, or demographic groups. One aggregate number can hide error patterns.

Use qualitative analysis

Inspect examples that succeed and fail. If metric differences are small on limited data, check statistical significance; consider human evaluation for generation tasks.

Test hypotheses, not just confirm them. Evaluation should try to rule out alternative explanations and expose cases where the model fails. Accuracy alone may ignore differences in error cost or fairness.

Efficiency is multidimensional: training cost, inference latency (time per response), throughput (responses per second), and memory requirements can trade off against quality. Report the dimension that matters for the application.

02 · Tokenization

Tokens are the units the model reads and emits

A token can be a whole word, a word part, punctuation, or a space-prefixed word. Generation chooses one token per model step, not necessarily one character or one word.

Characters

Small vocabulary, but long token sequences and many generation steps.

Whole words

Compact for common words, but vocabulary explodes and unseen words are a problem.

Subwords

A middle ground: frequent chunks become tokens; rare words split into pieces.

Watch a byte-pair merge build a new word

Byte Pair Encoding (BPE) begins with small units and repeatedly merges frequent adjacent pairs. The lecture’s toy corpus gives the pair e+s a high count, then merges adjacent pairs into chunks. A new word can be represented by replaying known merges even if the full word never appeared.

Toy word: “lowest”

The course assignment’s dataset is already tokenized (byte-level BPE with a finite vocabulary), so training a tokenizer is outside that implementation. The number and identity of tokens still affect context length and compute.

More tokens means more work

Each generated token typically triggers a sequential model step. During full-context attention, every token can compare to earlier tokens; the attention pair count grows approximately with T². Longer tokenization increases passes, attention pairs, cache size, and context-window usage.

03 · Decoding strategy

The same model can sound different with a different chooser

At each step, the model produces logits and therefore a distribution over the next token. The decoding algorithm chooses from it. Try the controls: they change which candidates remain and how likely each one is.

Fixed next-token logits

park 2.4 · town 1.8 · road 1.5 · people 0.7 · purple 0.1

Temperature rescales these before softmax.

Greedy decoding

Choose the most likely token at every step. Fast and deterministic, but a locally best choice can lead to repetitive or generic completions.

Beam search

Keep the top K partial sequences at each step, expanding and rescoring them by cumulative log probability. It explores several alternatives but costs more and can still favor odd, overlong, or generic sequences.

Most probable sequence ≠ most typical sequence. A locally normalized model can assign high sequence probability to a repetitive/generic output. Beam search can amplify degeneration. A biased-coin example illustrates this: the highest-probability long string is all heads, even though it may not represent a typical sample.
Truncation sampling

Sample after removing the unreliable tail

Pure sampling can select a very low-probability continuation from a long tail. Truncation methods remove candidates, renormalize the remaining probability mass, and sample among those.

Lower temperature sharpens the distribution; higher temperature flattens it. Temperature changes the relative probabilities before truncation. Top-k/top-p determine which options remain; multinomial sampling makes a stochastic choice.

04 · Beyond a single model call

Constrain, verify, or accelerate generation

Constrained decoding

Use a parser or constraint checker to remove tokens that would make the completion invalid. Examples include valid JSON, an email address, or including a required phrase. The algorithm only permits legal continuations.

Multiple models / verifiers

Combine model distributions (expert/anti-expert) or generate several candidates and score them with a verifier. A max strategy chooses the best verifier score; a weighted vote combines candidate support and verifier confidence.

Speculative decoding

A small fast model drafts several tokens. A large accurate model verifies the draft in parallel and accepts a prefix; after the first rejected guess, it corrects and continues. The target model preserves the intended output distribution while reducing serial target-model steps in favorable cases.

How to evaluate decoding

Measure task quality and efficiency: accuracy or human-rated quality, latency per response, throughput, and memory. A sampling strategy can improve diversity without changing the model parameters.

Revision check

Can you reason about tokens and choices?

Why can subword tokenization represent an unseen word?

It can replay learned subword merges; the word may split into familiar chunks or bytes instead of requiring a whole-word vocabulary entry.

If sequence length doubles, what happens to pairwise attention work?

It grows by about four times because the number of query-key pairs scales as T².

What does temperature τ=0.5 do compared with τ=1?

Dividing logits by a smaller value sharpens the softmax, concentrating probability more strongly on high-logit tokens.

How does top-p differ from top-k?

Top-k keeps a fixed number of candidates. Top-p keeps a variable number until their cumulative probability reaches p.

Why can greedy output be repetitive?

Each step chooses the local maximum; this can reinforce a high-probability loop even when the overall text is bland or degenerate.

What is the key trick in speculative decoding?

A cheap model proposes a multi-token draft and the large model verifies several proposed tokens in parallel, reducing serial large-model calls when guesses are accepted.

What’s the role of a development split?

Use it to choose models/hyperparameters; reserve the test split for the final estimate to avoid tuning on it.

Why examine errors by language or subgroup?

Aggregate metrics can hide unequal performance or failures on underrepresented populations and settings.
